Protecting Yourself from Fake Sites

Every platform in India's online gaming space has to deal with copycats — fake login pages, fake APK installers, fake "support" accounts on social media. They exist because the category moves money fast, which is exactly what scammers target. Knowing the patterns protects you on any platform, not just this one.

Before you enter your login details anywhere

Check the full URL, not just the logo on the page. You can check any DmFirst URL against the domain-verification tool atdmfirst.com— enter the domain you're on and it will show whether it matches DmFirst's current list of addresses. Do this before entering your login, depositing money, or adding bank details anywhere.

Common patterns to watch for

  • Fake APK mirrors.Android apps distributed outside the official site. These often install modified versions that capture login details. This site distributes the APK directly — third-party mirrors aren't verified by DmFirst.
  • Phishing links in SMS or WhatsApp.Messages claiming to be from the platform, usually offering a bonus, that link to a copy of the login page. Logging in there sends your credentials to the attacker.
  • Fake support accounts.Direct messages on Telegram, X, or Instagram claiming to be support. Real support never messages you first, and never asks for your password or OTP.
  • Platforms that block withdrawals after big wins.A known pattern across the category: the site accepts deposits, lets you build a balance, then freezes the account when you try to withdraw a large amount. If withdrawal terms seem unclear before you deposit, that's the risk.

What to do if something looks wrong

Don't enter any information. Close the page. Verify the URL through the domain check at dmfirst.com. If you've already entered credentials somewhere suspicious, change your password immediately — the OTP is what protects the reset.

Colour Prediction Rules

Colour prediction is the game most people visit DmFirst for. A round plays out like this:

  • You choose a colour — Green, Red, or Violet.
  • You stake an amount on that choice.
  • A timer runs down, usually 30 to 60 seconds.
  • A result is drawn. If your colour comes up, you get paid at the odds shown. If it doesn't, the stake is gone.

Green and Red usually pay 2x. Violet pays more because it appears less often. The odds are structured so the platform comes out ahead over thousands of rounds. Fine for entertainment. Not a way to earn a steady return.

What Counts as an Illegal Bet on DmFirst

An "illegal bet" isn't about how much you wager. It's about how you spread it across a round. DmFirst flags three specific patterns:

  • Betting both Big and Small in the same round
  • Betting both Red and Green in the same round
  • Betting on more than 8 of the 10 available numbers (0 through 9)

Any of these is treated as an illegal bet. The rule applies to WinGo, K3, 5D, and TRX WinGo — the specific version of it for each game lives in that game's "How to Play" section.

The reason it exists: without the rule, a player could cover both sides of a bet, hit the bonus threshold for a specific wagered amount, and collect extra credit meant to reward genuine play. The rule closes that gap.

If you trigger it, your withdrawal gets rejected with the comment "illegal bet," and the funds stay locked until support unlocks them. To unlock: contact support using the blue Customer Service icon at the bottom right of the app, request the unlock, then resubmit your withdrawal once the flag is cleared.
